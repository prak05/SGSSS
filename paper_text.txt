ABSTRACT Project management requires systematically planning and allocating time, resources, and
people to complete complex projects. A well-known problem in project management is the ResourceConstrained Project Scheduling Problem (RCPSP), which seeks to minimize total project duration
(makespan) while respecting limited resource availability and activity durations. The RCPSP is categorized
as an NP-hard problem, so no polynomial-time solution is known. Artificial intelligence is transforming
project management by automating and optimizing planning, execution, and control to boost project success.
In recent years, extensive research has been conducted on AI techniques aimed at improving project
task scheduling processes. In task scheduling, researchers employ nature-inspired metaheuristics, hyperheuristics, reinforcement learning, and hybrid techniques (e.g., GA, Bee algorithms, Simulated Annealing,
MDP/DRL approaches). The main contribution of the present study is the development of an artificial
intelligence model designed to select the most appropriate algorithm for project scheduling problems with
specific characteristics. This model consists of a neural network that takes as input the features of the project
and outputs a recommendation for the most suitable algorithm for scheduling the project under consideration.
The algorithms considered by the model include a Genetic Algorithm, a Simulated Annealing algorithm,
a Bee Colony Optimization algorithm, as well as a priority rule based heuristic algorithm. Each of these
methods offers distinct advantages depending on the nature of the project, such as its size, complexity,
constraints, and resource limitations. The AI model is trained to recognize which algorithm is likely to yield
the most effective scheduling results under different scenarios.
INDEX TERMS Neural networks, algorithmic selection, project scheduling, metaheuristics, optimization.

I. INTRODUCTION

The Resource-Constrained Project Scheduling Problem
(RCPSP) was first introduced by Pritsker in 1969 and has
since been extensively studied by numerous researchers. As a
well-known NP-hard problem [1] it belongs to the class
of non-deterministic polynomial-time problems, making it
inherently difficult to solve optimally. Due to this complexity, a wide range of metaheuristic algorithms have been
developed to approximate solutions to the problem, particularly in projects with many activities [2], [3]. Metaheuristic
algorithms designed for solving the RCPSP adopt various
operational strategies and can be categorized into three main
groups: local search-based metaheuristics (e.g., Simulated

solutions through the random application of priority rules.
The Genetic Algorithm was firstly introduced in the context of RCPSP by [17]. It is a population-based method that
leverages evolutionary theory, aiming to converge toward an
optimal or near-optimal solution through successive generations [18]. As one of the most prominent solution methods
for the RCPSP, Genetic Algorithm has undergone continuous
improvements. Notably, [19] introduced two scheduling tactics incorporating resource buffers, while [20] enhanced the
Genetic Algorithm by integrating alternative subgraphs and
applying a satisfiability.
The Simulated Annealing (SA) algorithm was initially
developed by [21], and later adapted for the RCPSP by [22].
SA belongs to the category of local search algorithms and
is inspired by physical processes, particularly the annealing
process in metallurgy [23].
The Bee Colony Optimization metaheuristic was originally proposed by [24]. Since then, several researchers,
including Shi et al. ([25]), as well as Akbari and Zaighami
[26], have modified this algorithm—also known as Artificial Bee Colony (ABC)—for application to the RCPSP.
The Bee Colony Optimization algorithm belongs to the
class of population-based algorithms and more specifically to the family of swarm intelligence optimization
techniques [27].
Learning-based metaheuristics have also emerged as a
powerful strategy for addressing optimization problems,
utilizing artificial intelligence and machine learning methods [28], [29]. These approaches typically consist of hybrid
algorithms that combine metaheuristic techniques with

machine learning and data mining methods to enhance the
capability and efficiency of optimization algorithms [28].
For the RCPSP, [30] proposed an artificial neural network
(ANN) model that generates project schedules by selecting
among three priority rules: Latest Finish Time (LFT), Minimum Slack Time (MST), and Maximum Remaining Path
Length (MRPL). Similarly, [31] introduced four ANN models, each with a different structure, to select activity priority
rules for the projects under consideration. Along the same
lines, [32] proposed a deep reinforcement learning (DRL)
model—specifically, a Graph Neural Network (GNN)—
which selects priority rules. Golab and Gooya also developed
ANN-based models, including a Multi-Layer Feedforward
Neural Network (MLFNN) and a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), that select activity priority rules based on
project features at each scheduling step [33], [34]. Finally,
[35] proposed a model based on reinforcement learning and
GNNs designed to map scheduling policies under conditions
of resource availability disruptions. The above studies highlight the increasing use of neural networks in addressing the
RCPSP, particularly in identifying appropriate priority rules.
Since priority rules are fundamental to project scheduling, the
academic community continues to publish research proposing new approaches and conducting further investigations in
this direction [36], [37], [38], [39].
Metaheuristic algorithms are powerful optimization tools,
designed to be problem-independent and therefore applicable
to a wide range of optimization problems. This broad applicability has led researchers to develop algorithm selection
methods. The aim of these methods is to identify the most
suitable algorithm for solving a given optimization problem,
rather than designing new algorithms for each specific case
[40]. The algorithm selection problem was first introduced by
[41], who defined a problem space and an algorithm space,
proposing that each problem-algorithm pair can be mapped
based on performance. This mapping enables the selection
of the most effective algorithm for a specific problem. Since
then, algorithm selection models have been developed for a
variety of optimization problems, including bin packing, the
competitive travelling salesman problem, exam timetabling,
and flow shop scheduling [40].
For the RCPSP specifically, [42] proposed an automatic
selection approach that chooses between two algorithms.
This method effectively forms a hyper-heuristic, which performs better than either of the two algorithms when used
individually.

III. THE CONCEPT OF NEURAL NETWORK FOR
EVALUATING AND SELECTING METAHEURISTICS

Neural networks are computational models inspired by
the structure and function of the human brain, consisting of interconnected neurons organized in layers. This
architecture enables neural networks to learn patterns and
discover relationships within large datasets, in a manner similar to the human brain. This ability is utilized
in the present study to evaluate the results of metaheuristic algorithms and to recommend the most suitable
algorithm based on the characteristics of the project under
consideration.
While previous approaches have been focused on the
selection mechanism between two algorithms or the development of entirely new novel algorithms, the method proposed in this work extends the idea by employing an
Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to choose among four
distinct metaheuristic algorithms. Unlike the earlier studies the algorithms considered here differ in their operational strategies, thereby broadening the search landscape.
This not only increases the flexibility of the selection
mechanism but also enhances its potential to adapt to a
wider variety of problem instances. Also, unlike traditional
hyper-heuristics that focus on selecting priority rules or
constructing schedules, the proposed model selects among
diverse metaheuristic search strategies based on project
topology.

The resource factor (RF) refers to the average percentage

B. THE PROPOSED APPROACH

The outputs of the neural network correspond to four distinct
algorithms selected to represent diverse search strategies for
solving the RCPSP: a Genetic Algorithm (GA), a Simulated Annealing algorithm (SA), a Bee Colony Optimization
algorithm (BCO), and a Priority Rule-Based Heuristic (PRH).
Instead of designing novel metaheuristics, this study employs
well-established standard versions of these algorithms to
focus on the efficacy of the selection model.
While implementing the metaheuristic algorithms, we used
the random key representation, which is based on the concept
of prioritizing activities. Random key representation employs
a real random number r ∈ [0, 1], which serves as the
priority value for every activity of the project. To generate
solutions for the metaheuristic algorithms, we employed the
serial scheduling generation scheme (SSGS) proposed by
Kelley [48]. This method was selected because of its superiority over the parallel method for creating schedules [3], [48].
The operational procedures of the algorithms are described
below:
Simulated Annealing (SA) [49] represents a trajectorybased metaheuristic that avoids local optima through a
VOLUME 14, 2026

N. T. Mitsou, G. K. Koulinas: AI-Enhanced Metaheuristics Control for Project Scheduling

probabilistic cooling mechanism. The Genetic Algorithm
(GA) [50] is an evolutionary, population-based method utilizing selection, crossover, and mutation operators. Bee Colony
Optimization [51] relies on swarm intelligence principles,
employing scout and recruit bees to balance exploration and
exploitation. Finally, the priority rule based heuristic (PRH)
is a constructive heuristic that employs the Serial Schedule
Generation Scheme (SSGS) combined with standard priority
rules (EST, LST, EFT, LFT) or random selection to generate
schedules efficiently.
To ensure a fair comparison across these diverse methods,
all algorithms were initialized to generate an equivalent total
number of schedules (set to 5,000 schedules per instance).
For the single-solution approaches (SA and PRH), this corresponded directly to the number of iterations. For the
population-based methods (GA and BCO), the population
size and number of generations were adjusted to match this
total evaluation budget. This standardization ensures that any
performance differences are attributable to the search strategy
rather than computational resource disparities.
Finally, in this study, we employed metaheuristic algorithms based on different solution methods to provide insights
not only into the most effective algorithm, but also into the
most appropriate search strategy. For instance, the Genetic
Algorithm is a population-based method that leverages evolutionary concepts. Similarly, the Bees Algorithm is also
population-based but draws on principles of swarm intelligence. In contrast, Simulated Annealing is a trajectory-based
algorithm. Lastly, the priority rule based heuristic algorithm
does not belong to the metaheuristic category but is classified as a heuristic [52]. This diversity of algorithms ensures
that instead of developing entirely new methods to solve
the RCPSP, the model can recommend the most appropriate
existing algorithm for a given project instance. Consequently,
the user is empowered either to directly apply the suggested
algorithm or to explore alternative algorithms that operate on
the same underlying search mechanism (e.g., evolutionary,
swarm intelligence, etc.).

IV. APPLICATION TO THE PSPLIB SET OF PROBLEMS

The algorithms generating neural network training data
include Simulated Annealing (SA), Bee Colony Optimization
(BCO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Priority Rule-Based
Heuristic (PRH). These solve RCPSP instances in PSPLIB
and provide data for the FNN. We select PSPLIB projects
with 60 and 120 activities.

N. T. Mitsou, G. K. Koulinas: AI-Enhanced Metaheuristics Control for Project Scheduling

PSPLIB is a library of project data generated through
the ProGen generator. The projects have been created based
on specific characteristics and are grouped accordingly. The
characteristics of the projects used for training the neural
network consist of the number of activities (N), network
complexity (NC), resource factor (RF), and resource strength
(RS) [47].
The dataset was constructed through the following process:
Initially, project data from PSPLIB were grouped based on
the number of activities. Within each activity group, projects
are further organized into subsets of ten projects based on
common values of Network Complexity (NC), Resource Factor (RF), and Resource Strength (RS). Each algorithm is
provided with project subsets that share the same characteristics. The objective is for each algorithm to independently
identify the optimal solution. This process enables a comparative evaluation of each algorithm’s performance across
projects with identical network characteristics. Analyzing the
algorithms’ results allows for evaluation of potential correlations between specific algorithms and the quality of the
solutions obtained.
If all algorithms identified the same solution in a project
subset, further comparison could use secondary criteria,
such as shorter processing time or computational efficiency.
To ensure mutually exclusive classes for the classification
task and avoid label ambiguity during training, a strict hierarchy was applied. The ALL class (Class 5) was assigned if all
algorithms score identical makespans. In such cases, the specific algorithm labels were suppressed, and the instance was
strictly categorized as ‘ALL’. This distinction is critical as it
trains the network to recognize project topological features
that render the specific choice of metaheuristic negligible,
distinct from cases where a specific algorithm provides a
measurable advantage.
A critical preprocessing step involved the resolution of
label conflicts to ensure dataset consistency. Since the input
features (NC, RF, RS) serve as high-level descriptors of
project topology, it is possible for distinct project instances to
share identical feature vectors while initially having different
optimal algorithms due to latent structural differences. Such
instances create ‘one-to-many’ mappings (conflicting labels)
that introduce noise and hinder neural network convergence.
To eliminate ambiguity and ensure a deterministic mapping, the dataset underwent a rigorous refinement process.
For project instances where multiple algorithms achieved the
same optimal objective value, a tie-breaking rule was applied.
Specifically, the instance was labeled based on the algorithm
that demonstrated the superior aggregate performance across
the entire set of ten projects for that specific configuration.
This approach resolved potential label noise by assigning
a single, consistent target to each feature vector, allowing
the PCFNN to learn a well-defined classification boundary.
The robust validation accuracy achieved further confirms
that the model has captured meaningful patterns rather than
overfitting, a result validated by the 10-fold cross-validation
scheme.

A. HYPERPARAMETER DERIVATION AND MODEL
SELECTION

The architectural characteristics and hyperparameters of the
proposed neural network were not manually tuned but were
systematically derived through the MATLAB Classification
Learner framework. To ensure optimal performance and generalization, we utilized the Optimizable Neural Network
module, which automates the selection of model parameters.
To ensure the robustness of the proposed model and the
reliability of the reported metrics, a 10-fold cross-validation
scheme was implemented. This methodology partitions the
dataset into ten disjoint subsets, the model is iteratively
trained on nine subsets and validated on the tenth. This process is repeated ten times, ensuring that every data point is
used for both training and validation. This rigorous approach
confirms that the high accuracy achieved is not a result of
overfitting and that the model generalizes effectively across
the entire dataset. To eliminate any potential data leakage, the
10-fold cross-validation was performed at the configuration
level rather than at the individual instance level. This ensures
that instances sharing identical features were consistently
kept together in either the training or validation folds.
The hyperparameter tuning process was driven by a
Bayesian Optimization algorithm. This approach treats
selection as a derivative-free optimization problem, iteratively exploring the parameter space to minimize validation
error. Specifically, the model evaluated various configurations involving the number of fully connected layers,
hidden layer sizes, activation functions, and the regularization
strength λ to prevent overfitting.

we ensured that the resulting model configurations represent

V. APPLICATION IN A REAL CONSTRUCTION PROJECT

VI. CONCLUSION

In this paper, we present a classification feedforward neural
network (PCFNN), an artificial intelligence model developed to address the Resource-Constrained Project Scheduling
Problem (RCPSP). This model focuses on identifying and
recommending the most suitable algorithm within a predefined set for solving RCPSP instances. The advantages of
using this model include the fact that it is trained only once
to adjust the neural network weights, after which it can recommend optimal algorithms from the set on which it was
trained. The alternative approach involves applying multiple
algorithms and comparing their results to select the best one.
Therefore, this model saves time for the user and guides the
RCPSP solution towards specific algorithms. Results show
that for projects with 60 activities, the model predicted the
optimal algorithm with 98.4% accuracy, and 92.4% accuracy
for those with 120 activities. In both the 60- and 120-activity
project sets, the algorithm that consistently produced the best
solutions was the Genetic Algorithm. While this algorithmic dominance highlights the effectiveness of the Genetic
Algorithm, it also introduces a natural class imbalance within
the dataset, requiring careful data handling to ensure the
model reliably identifies the minority classes. The superior
performance of this algorithm compared to the metaheuristic algorithms for projects with 60 and 120 activities may
be attributed to the vast solution space, where the Genetic
Algorithm effectively targeted regions with better solutions
compared to the others. This observation, however, warrants
further investigation.
The developed PCFNN is designed to predict the optimal
algorithm for RCPSP instances with project characteristics
and activity numbers identical to those found in the PSPLIB
library, as the neural network was trained specifically with
this dataset. This poses a potential limitation regarding
the model’s broader generalization to completely unknown
project characteristics. To further enhance their predictive
capability and generalization, future research is needed that
involves training them on project generators with a broader
combination of project characteristics as well as varying
numbers of activities. Despite this fact the PCFNN managed
to accurately predict the correct algorithm for solving the
case study, even though its characteristics were different
from those used for its training. Although the model can
recommend only four algorithms, we believe this approach
encourages researchers to use its features and develop similar
models for algorithm comparison and recommendations in
RCPSP solutions.
